The 100 AI Customers Kit

Test your next salon service on 100 AI customers in Claude before you spend a dollar on flyers, equipment or training. Three copy-paste prompts and one checklist.

The trap: AI customers are people-pleasers. Ask "would you buy this?" and they say yes to almost anything. So never test one idea: make them choose between three, and answer in words, not scores.
PROMPT 1

Build your 100 customers

Paste into a new Claude chat. Swap the orange parts for your salon.

I own [a mid-priced neighbourhood hair salon in a US suburb]. Before I spend money launching a new service, I want to test it on 100 realistic local customers.

Create 100 customer personas who could realistically walk into my salon. Make them as varied as real clients: different ages, incomes, jobs, hair types and habits. Include budget clients, loyal regulars, people who only come twice a year, and people who never pay for extras.

Write them in 4 batches of 25 so every person stays distinct. Output a table with these columns:
# | Name | Age | Job | Monthly hair budget | How often they book | What they pay now per visit | What annoys them about salons
PROMPT 2

The head-to-head test

Same chat. Put in your three ideas with real prices.

Now every one of the 100 customers sees these three offers:
A) [Blowout Membership: $79/month for 4 blowouts]
B) [Bridal Package: $350 for a trial run + wedding-day styling]
C) [Scalp Treatment: $35 add-on to any appointment]

Rules:
- Shuffle the order of A, B and C for every customer, so no offer is always shown first.
- Stay in character. Use each customer's age, budget and habits.
- Each customer picks the ONE offer they would actually pay for.
- In their own words (1–2 sentences), they say why they'd walk away from the other two.
- No scores, no 1–10 ratings, no "would you buy this?". Real customers say no to most things, so be honest.

Work in 4 batches of 25. Output a table:
# | Name | Order shown | Pick | Why they'd walk away from the other two
PROMPT 3

The tally

Same chat. Claude builds a real .xlsx you can download (file creation is on paid Claude plans).

Tally all 100 answers into a spreadsheet (.xlsx) with 3 tabs:
1. "Results": each offer, number of picks, % of picks. Highlight the winner.
2. "Walk-away reasons": group the reasons into the top 5 reasons people said no, with a count and one real quote each.
3. "Fix before launch": for the winning offer, what to change before launch, based on what people said.

Before you print a flyer

  • Test the winner on 10 real clients before you print a flyer.
  • Ask them in the chair. Show the offer and the price, then ask what would stop them booking it.
  • Write down their words, not a score, and compare them with the AI walk-away reasons.
  • Fix what they flag first. AI customers are for early testing; real clients have the final say.

The offers and prices above are examples. Swap in your own. AI customers are a fast first filter, not a replacement for real clients. Research on AI survey answers: NN/g on synthetic users · Maier et al. 2025 (arXiv preprint).
